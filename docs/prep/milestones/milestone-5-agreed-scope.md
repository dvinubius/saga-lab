# Milestone 5 — Agreed scope

Build specification: [GitHub issue #54 — Complete visitor experience: top-ups, outcome summary and playback](https://github.com/dvinubius/saga-lab/issues/54).

These decisions refine milestone 5. Everything in the milestone 5 entry in [Milestones](../saga-lab-04-milestones.md) is in scope. Cookie-associated visitors, visitor-owned accounts and all five scenarios already shipped in milestone 4.

- **Bank names:** generated bank display names are dropped from V1, amending section 2 of the [Application Requirements](../saga-lab-01-application-requirements.md). Every page says Bank A and Bank B.
- **Top-ups:** a fixed 100 credits added to the visitor's Bank A account. Bank A owns the amount, as it owns the opening balance. The Transfer Service calls Bank A's internal `POST /accounts/{visitorID}/top-ups` synchronously, which returns the new balance. A top-up is not a transfer: it sends no message and writes no history. There is no request deduplication; a double click adds 200 fictional credits. While a transfer is pending, including one awaiting admission, a top-up is refused, accepting the check-then-call race. The home page's Bank A card holds a secondary "+100 credits" button posting `POST /top-ups` and redirecting home, disabled with the existing pending note while a transfer is pending. The JSON API offers `POST /api/top-ups`, returning the new balances, or 409 naming the pending transfer as the pending-transfer restriction does.
- **Balances in evidence:** each bank outcome event (FundsDebited, DebitRejected, FundsCredited, CreditRejected, FundsRefunded) carries the visitor's balance at that bank before and after its own effect; a rejection reports an unchanged pair. The Transfer Service stores the pair on the step's history entry, in nullable columns with no backfill. A committed step shows its change ("100 → 75") in History and in the playback panel.
- **Outcome summary:** shown once the replay is ready, between the playback panel and History. Bank A's before comes from the debit step and its after from the last Bank A step; Bank B's pair comes from its credit or rejection step, and Bank B is "not involved" after a debit rejection. A transfer without recorded pairs shows "—". The same table for every scenario: per command (debit, credit, refund) the handling attempts, counted as distinct attempt IDs among entries caused by that command, and the committed business effects, counted as committed steps for that command; a command never issued shows "—". Below it, duplicates suppressed and duplicate effects (effects beyond one per command), derived from history and never asserted. `GET /api/transfers/{id}` carries the same computed value as an `outcome` object once ready.
- **Phases:** the Status row already distinguishes admission waiting from Saga execution. A Replay row replaces the Evidence row: "Available once the transfer has ended" while pending, "Being prepared" once the transfer has ended but its evidence is incomplete, "Ready" once ready. While not ready, the page keeps its one-second reload and shows the live History table without a playback panel.
- **Playback:** a panel above the History table, which stays the complete record. Small client-side script steps through the server-rendered History rows, which carry their timestamps in `data-` attributes; there is no client renderer fed by JSON. Each row is one playback step. The panel shows the current entry's label and note, its real timestamp and the real gap to the next entry, and a diagram of four nodes: Transfer Service, Broker, Bank A and Bank B. It autoplays once only when the replay becomes ready while the page is open; a page loaded on an already ready transfer, including an old one opened from the list, rests paused on the first step. Controls: play/pause, step back, step forward and replay. Stepping pauses. At the end it rests on the last step with every row played, and play becomes "Replay". No speed control and no keyboard shortcuts beyond native button focus.
- **Diagram highlights:** a step caused by a message lights its path sender → Broker → receiver ("Bank A committed the debit": Transfer Service → Broker → Bank A). "Transfer requested", "admitted" and "broker confirmed command": Transfer Service → Broker. "Command delivery waiting": Broker only, with Bank B marked in text as having no consumer. "Command delivered": Broker → Bank B. "Couldn't acknowledge after commit": Bank A → Broker. "Redelivery rejected; nothing applied": Broker → Bank A. The highlight is a static path, never a moving mark that would suggest measured network latency.
- **Styling:** idle diagram nodes and edges are muted, lit ones body text, and the travelled path Teal. In History the current row takes `--surface-shade`, unplayed rows drop to `--disabled-opacity`, played rows are normal; the scenarios' Brick and Teal entry marks stay. The design system's no-motion rule is removed in the same change.
- **Timing:** each row dwells for the real gap from its timestamp to the next timestamped row, clamped to 700 ms–4 s, so millisecond steps show for 700 ms and the broker wait for 4 s. The synthetic "Command delivery waiting" row borrows the broker confirmation's timestamp, so the confirmation dwells 700 ms and the waiting row carries the wait. A negative gap from out-of-order observations clamps to 700 ms; real timestamps are always shown unchanged. A long admission wait caps at 4 s and its real length stays in the text.
- **Primary test boundary:** over HTTP against the running stack: a top-up raises the Bank A balance and is refused while a transfer is pending; each scenario's outcome summary carries the expected balances and counts (debit redelivery: debit 2 attempts and 1 effect, credit 1 and 1, duplicate effects 0). Package-level tests cover the counts derivation and the dwell computation. The playback panel and diagram get an eyeball checklist, not browser tests.
